# Session recap — 2026-07-14: inc-CLK-1 (block timestamp, inert additive) + the Council catch

Plain-English recap of a full-loop increment. HEAD `32b87b5`, pushed.

## What we set out to do
Continue the committee-attested-clock arc. Last session shipped the clock's *validation kernel*
(`wallclock.rs`) as an isolated, tested module that touches nothing. inc-CLK-1 was meant to start
*wiring* it toward consensus: give a block a wall-clock `timestamp`, and connect the pieces so a future
difficulty retarget can read real elapsed time.

## How we did it — THE LOOP
Ran your full method: **2 planners** (one mechanism lens, one build-safety lens, in parallel) →
**build** (RED-first) → **stress test** → **Council** (3 adversarial reviewers, read-only) →
**Pragma confluence** (code ≡ docs) → commit. The two planners converged hard — same central hazard,
same fix — which was a good early signal.

## The Council earned its keep — a real bug, caught before commit
The first cut shipped a **monotonicity rule** as a live consensus rule (a block's timestamp can't run
backward vs the last one) — but its **magnitude guard** (the node-local "is this timestamp close to my
own clock?" check) was deferred to a later increment. All three reviewers independently found the
consequence: a single block with `timestamp = u64::MAX` would be accepted (it's "forward"), pin the
comparand at the maximum, and then **reject every real timestamp forever** — a permanent brick of the
clock channel from one block. Worse, the field was off the state-digest, so two nodes could silently
disagree about it.

**Why it happened (the lesson):** the monotonicity rule and the magnitude guard are *interdependent* —
the ordering rule is only safe if the magnitude is bounded. I split them across two increments. That is
exactly the inverse of the design law we wrote earlier today: *only decouple things that are genuinely
independent.* I decoupled two interdependent things, and the gap between them was the bug.

## The fix — dissolve the class, don't patch the case
Instead of bolting on a bound, we collapsed inc-CLK-1 back to **pure inert additive data** — the exact
precedent from when PoW landed (the seal was carried as inert data in M2a-1, then enforced in M2a-2).
So inc-CLK-1 now ships:
- the `timestamp` field, carried on the wire (old block logs still decode, as `None`);
- the admission-check *logic* as a pure, tested helper (ready to wire, wired to nothing yet);
- the "elapsed time → retarget" seam, proven by test;
- **no enforcement at all** — nothing in consensus reads the timestamp, so there is nothing to poison.

The whole enforcement bundle — the monotonicity rule, the magnitude guard wired at the node's live
ingress, the header binding, and the actual retarget activation — now ships **together, as one unit**,
in inc-CLK-2, where it belongs (with the numbers you still need to ratify).
